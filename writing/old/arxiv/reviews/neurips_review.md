# NeurIPS 2026 Applications Track — Reviewer Report

**Paper:** *Simulator-Interface Grounding Adapters for Scientific Simulation Setup: A Geophysics Case Study*

---

## 1. Summary

The paper studies whether four simple, well-known wrapper-level interventions — retrieval (R), a stop-hook validator (S), an agent-callable XML linter (X), and a system-prompt cheatsheet (M) — can turn a frontier coding harness (Claude Code on deepseek-v4-flash) into a usable assistant for GEOS multiphysics simulation deck authoring. The headline empirical artifacts are (i) a Resolution-IV $2^{4-1}$ factorial on a 17-task validation split and a 10-task harder held-out split, scored by a tree-edit similarity metric "TreeSim"; (ii) a self-evolved variant whose adapter content was offline-rewritten against the training split; (iii) a small companion study on under-specification and a `consult_supervisor` MCP tool; (iv) a 2-participant human baseline on a single task; and (v) a 5-task OpenFOAM transfer study against a Foam-Agent baseline restricted to its lint-only execution mode. The authors find that on the in-distribution validation set everything sits inside seed noise, but on the harder held-out set the best configuration yields roughly +7 pp mean TreeSim and roughly 40× lower across-seed variance, with most of the lift coming from preventing zero-score (unparseable / empty / missing-file) failures on a handful of compound multi-physics tasks. The conclusion is framed as a "harm-reduction, not correctness" picture: schema-driven adapters fix block-level omissions but leave attribute-level errors untouched, and the OpenFOAM transfer reproduces the same qualitative pattern with the stop-hook again carrying most of the weight.

## 2. Strengths

- **Honest empirical framing in the abstract and discussion.** The abstract explicitly says SIGA "improves reliability… by preventing unparseable or empty decks on a *hard tail*" and the conclusion says the system operates "in a harm-reduction regime, not a correctness regime." These are unusually candid statements for an Applications submission and are well supported by the per-task breakdown (Table 4, App. C).
- **Two cautionary negative findings that are independently useful.** Section 5.2 (memory-as-retrieval: an MCP `memory_lookup` tool exposed to the agent was called zero times, while the same content delivered via `--append-system-prompt` produced the lift) and §5.4 (the `consult_supervisor` channel was invoked in only 2/64 trials, with the agent substituting the on-disk example library as a cheaper oracle) are concrete, falsifiable design lessons that other agent-systems work would benefit from. The Mandel/Hard diagnostic (15 of 26 dropped values literally `grep`-able in other readable GEOS examples) gives the second finding a credible mechanism.
- **Bottleneck decomposition is well-executed.** The LLM-classifier pipeline (App. C) and the per-cell category counts (Table 3) ground the abstract "missing\_block vs bad\_attribute\_value" claim in numbers, and the discussion's adapter-design recommendations are explicitly tied to surviving failure categories.
- **Explicit hygiene discipline.** App. A.1 documents an earlier cheatsheet that leaked 13/17 validation-task basenames; the authors added a regex hygiene gate and re-anchored to v2 specs after v1/v2 contamination (App. A.2). Disclosing prior contamination rather than hiding it is exactly the right behaviour.
- **The native-plugin-prefix bug write-up (App. F).** Authors discovered, via cross-model trajectory inspection, that a leftover system-prompt prefix was naming RAG tool prefixes verbatim and producing a 0.392 minimax × X+M result with 8/17 task failures. The fix raised it to 0.867. Disclosing this rather than silently dropping the contaminated run is a strong process signal.
- **Reasonable scoping of contribution.** The contributions list does not claim methodological novelty; it claims an evaluation, a component-by-component design analysis, two cautionary findings, and a small transfer study. This matches what the empirical material can support.
- **Broad and credible related-work coverage.** §2 cites adjacent simulator-agent work across chemistry, CFD, MD, FEM, and reservoir simulation, plus general harnesses (OpenHands, SWE-agent) and the procedural-memory line. The Foam-Agent comparator choice is appropriate even if the execution mode is constrained.

## 3. Weaknesses (prioritized — most damaging first)

### W1. The headline effect rests on two tasks. [Severity: high]
The +0.069 Vanilla→SE delta on held-out-eval is concentrated in **two** of the ten tasks (Table 4: `AdvancedExampleThermoPoroElasticWellbore` 0.355→0.761, `ExampleProppantTest` 0.541→0.825). A third task (`TutorialHydraulicFractureWithAdvancedXML`) is at 0.013 across every cell — i.e., universally broken and irrelevant for ranking. On the remaining seven tasks the Vanilla→SE per-task differences are −0.038, +0.037, +0.035, +0.006, −0.083, −0.005, and +0.010. The aggregate effect is therefore a small-n binary outcome dressed up as a mean: "did the system rescue these two specific compound tasks?" That is an interesting *case study* but it is not an effect size that survives a different two-task draw. The authors acknowledge this in §5.1 ("hard-tail effect"), but the abstract still leads with "+$7$ percentage points" and "$40\times$" framing that lands very differently than "we rescued 2 of 10 held-out tasks from catastrophic-failure regimes." A reviewer-friendly version of this section would report the per-task win/loss matrix in the main body and replace the aggregate-only language in the abstract.

### W2. The 40× reliability claim is fragile. [Severity: high]
The 40× number is $0.081 / 0.002$ — i.e., the ratio of Vanilla's held-out-eval std to S+X's held-out-eval std at $n=3$ seeds. Both standard deviations are computed from three samples, on ten tasks, with the numerator dominated by a single zero-score seed on `ExampleProppantTest` (§5.1). The ratio is therefore the ratio of two estimates each with a ~70% sampling CI under standard $\chi^2$ assumptions; the true ratio could be anywhere from ~5× to ~200×. The absolute numbers also matter: a std of 0.081 on a metric in $[0,1]$ across $n=3$ seeds and 10 tasks is not "unreliable in any meaningful operational sense"; it is "one seed once produced an unparseable file." Reporting bootstrap or block-bootstrap CIs on the std *ratio*, or reporting the underlying counts ("Vanilla produced 1 zero-score seed; S+X produced 0") would make this honest. The "40×" framing is the kind of round number that will follow this paper into citations and lose all of its caveats.

### W3. The SE comparison is not apples-to-apples. [Severity: medium-high]
SE rewrites the adapter contents offline against held-out-train TreeSim (§3.1, "iteratively rewrites the adapter contents… against held-out training-set TreeSim"). The hand-designed S+X+M cell got no such offline tuning. SE therefore consumed an extra optimization budget (over the 18 distillation tasks) that the hand-designed cell did not. On val, SE = 0.919 ± 0.020 vs S+X+M = 0.911 ± 0.018 — overlapping. On held-out-eval, SE = 0.789 ± 0.012 vs S+X+M = 0.783 ± 0.022 — overlapping. The headline phrase "matches the best hand-designed cell with roughly $16\%$ fewer tool calls" is val-only (efficiency table, App. M: SE 68.9 vs Vanilla 81.5 tools/task on val — but on held-out-eval SE makes 97.4 tools/task vs Vanilla's 90.5, i.e. *more* tools). The claim that "automatic SIGA discovery is tractable" is overstated by a result where the autotuned variant matches a hand-tuned variant within seed noise after consuming a training set the hand-tuned variant did not see.

### W4. The human baseline cannot support the "8×–36× faster" framing. [Severity: medium-high]
$n{=}2$, single task (the easy end of the bench), both participants timed out at 47–48 min and submitted only one of the two required files. The "more than 8× as long" number in the abstract divides the 1-hour budget by the agent's ~7 min wall time — but the 1-hour budget is the cap that triggered the truncation, not an observed completion time, so the comparison is between (a) a deck the agent completed and (b) a deck the human did not complete. The deck-level number for the 1h human is 0.540/0.527; for Vanilla CC it is 0.751. The 36× number divides P1's no-cap 180 min by the agent's 5 min and reaches 0.931 — but the supplementary disclosure that P1 visited ChatGPT once during a post-authoring cluster-debugging step (App. K) is exactly the kind of single-data-point integrity issue a reviewer will note. The honest framing is "two domain experts new to GEOS struggle to complete a two-file deck in one hour"; that does not justify a multiplicative speedup claim in the abstract.

### W5. Methodological novelty is essentially zero, and the framing does not fully own this. [Severity: medium]
The "method" is four well-known interventions (retrieval, stop-hook, validator, system-prompt cheatsheet) plus a self-evolution loop, all wrapped around an existing harness. The abstract markets this as "Simulator-Interface Grounding Adapter (SIGA)" and the conclusion calls it a "definition" of SIGA. For an Applications-track submission this can be fine, but the paper needs to either (a) own that the contribution is the *empirical study* of those four interventions on a specific scientific domain, in which case the abstract should foreground the bottleneck analysis and the negative findings, not the +7pp / 40× numbers; or (b) defend the SIGA *name* as a useful taxonomic contribution, which it does not currently do. As written, the paper is borderline overselling a brand for what is, in implementation, a wrapper script and a cheatsheet.

### W6. The OpenFOAM transfer study has multiple structural concerns. [Severity: medium]
- Single seed on 5 tasks. The R+X cell scoring 0.145 (4 of 5 tasks at zero) on a single seed is a single-seed catastrophe that any other cell on a single seed could also have experienced; it is presented as a property of the cell rather than as a possible bad draw.
- Foam-Agent was crippled to lint-only because "execute-mode runs failed to yield usable benchmark outputs in our environment" (App. G, "Foam-Agent baseline"). This is exactly the configuration that biases the comparison in the proposed system's favour. The paper acknowledges this once and then claims that "the best OpenFOAM SIGA cell reaches $0.871$ versus… $0.569$ for the constrained baseline" — but the magnitude of that gap is partly an artifact of the constraint. The follow-ups appendix lists a multi-seed execute-mode rerun (App. H) as future work; until that is done, the +0.302 Δ-vs-Foam-Agent should not be in the abstract or contributions.
- The metric is different from TreeSim (file-text-and-coverage with a 0.7/0.3 weighting). The single-seed factor-style readout ($R{:}{-}0.050$, $S{:}{+}0.328$, $X{:}{-}0.073$, $M{:}{+}0.192$) is calculated on $n=8$ cells with $n=1$ seed each. These are descriptive numbers; presenting them as a "factor-style readout" gives them more weight than the design supports.

### W7. The R main effect on val is *also* contaminated and the discussion underplays this. [Severity: medium]
App. F discloses that the native-plugin-prefix bug contaminated R$^-$ cells in the cross-model panel, and the limitations paragraph says it "contaminated the $R = -0.033$ estimate, but the X+M-vs-Vanilla ranking is unaffected since both are R$^-$." That logic is correct for the X+M-vs-Vanilla ranking, but §5.1 ("On val, adapter wins fall within seed noise") still says "the only main effect that clearly exceeds run-to-run variability is generic retrieval; however, it decreases performance." If R$^-$ cells were contaminated by extraneous text in the user prompt that named non-registered tools, the R main effect on val is exactly the quantity that should not be reported as a finding without a clean rerun. The "Clean re-run" in the follow-ups appendix is the right action but it should be a precondition for the published R claim, not a follow-up.

### W8. "Failures-as-zero" reporting may overstate effect sizes. [Severity: low-medium]
TreeSim is in $[0,1]$ and missing / unparseable outputs are scored 0. This is defensible. But it means that a single missing file on a single seed can swing a cell mean by ~0.07 on $n=3$ seeds and 10 tasks, which is exactly the magnitude of the headline +0.069 effect. The mean-with-failures-as-zero metric is therefore largely a measure of "did the cell ever produce an empty / unparseable output," dressed as a continuous quality score. A useful additional column would report the mean TreeSim *conditional on a parseable output* alongside the failure rate, so the reader can separate "reliability" from "quality" cleanly.

### W9. Audience fit / venue. [Severity: low-medium]
This is an honest, careful empirical study of wrapper-level interventions for a niche simulator. Its strongest readers are probably authors of other simulator-specific agent papers (Foam-Agent, MOOSEAgent, MD-Agent2, JutulGPT, …) or the GEOS community itself. The methodological lessons (retrieve-vs-prepend; consultation-vs-substitutes) generalise, but the empirical bulk is GEOS-XML. A reviewer reasonably asks: is the NeurIPS Applications audience well-served, or is this a paper for a domain workshop? The bottleneck analysis and the procedural-memory / consultation findings are the strongest answers to that question. The OpenFOAM transfer study should help here, but in its current single-seed-5-task form it is closer to "anecdote" than "transfer evidence."

### W10. Writing / framing. [Severity: low]
- The system name "SIGA" appears 30+ times and is introduced as a "package of skills, tools, and workflow control flow." It does not denote any specific algorithmic content — the same four levers under any other name would yield the same study. Either define SIGA crisply (e.g., as "the four-component wrapper of {R, S, X, M}") or drop the branded name.
- The introduction does not signal Applications-track framing clearly enough. The contribution list reads as a methods paper. A one-sentence "we treat this as an empirical study; the contribution is in the evaluation, not in the components" would help reviewers.
- The "SE" / "SE-prose" distinction needs a cleaner one-sentence statement of what SE-prose is meant to *test* relative to SE; right now the reader has to assemble it from §3.1.

## 4. Questions for the authors

1. Per-task ranks for held-out-eval: can you provide a sign-test or rank-test across the 10 held-out-eval tasks comparing SE vs Vanilla, treating each task as one observation? The aggregate effect being concentrated in two tasks invites a non-parametric reading.
2. The 40× variance ratio is computed from $n=3$ seeds. Could you provide a bootstrap CI for the ratio, and report the underlying counts ("k seeds out of N produced a zero-score output for each cell")?
3. The SE pipeline used the 18 distillation tasks for offline rewriting. Were SE's adapter-version selections evaluated against the val split before final SE was frozen? If yes, the val numbers for SE are partly a model-selection result rather than a held-out result.
4. For the human baseline, can you report the agent's *wall* time under the same one-hour budget rather than its observed completion time? The "8× faster" framing reads as "the agent fits in the budget; the humans did not" — which is a finding, but a different one from "the agent is 8× faster."
5. On the consultation finding: did the agent ever attempt to use the supervisor channel and abandon? I.e., is the 3.1% an "asked once and gave up" rate, or a "never tried" rate? The events.jsonl data should answer this.
6. Foam-Agent execute-mode failures: what was the failure mode? Did Foam-Agent crash, time out, or produce unscorable outputs? A one-paragraph diagnosis would clarify whether the lint-only configuration is a fair constrained baseline or a degenerate one.
7. The "memory-as-retrieval" negative result: was the `memory_lookup` MCP tool's description visible to the agent in the same way as other MCP tools? Is there a confound where the system-prompt cheatsheet primes specific tokens that the retrieval index does not?
8. On the R main effect of −0.032 on val: how much of this estimate survives once R$^-$ cells whose prefix referenced non-registered tools are removed?
9. App. M efficiency table: SE has 68.9 tools/task on val (15.5% fewer than Vanilla's 81.5) but 97.4 on held-out-eval (7.6% *more* than Vanilla's 90.5). Why does the "16% fewer tool calls" headline reference val only?
10. Did the cross-model panel's "X+M vs Vanilla" advantage on minimax-m2.7 ($0.867$ vs $0.821$, $+0.046$ at $n=1$) replicate when re-run? A second seed on minimax would close a real gap.

## 6. Overall recommendation

**Borderline Reject.**

The paper is honest, the bottleneck analysis is competent, and the two cautionary findings (procedural-memory-as-retrieval, consultation-vs-substitutes) are genuinely useful for the simulator-agent community. But the headline empirical numbers are concentrated in 2 of 10 held-out-eval tasks at $n=3$ seeds, the 40× variance claim is one-zero-score-seed deep, the SE comparison consumed extra offline training budget that the hand-designed cells did not, the human baseline cannot support an 8–36× speedup framing, and the OpenFOAM transfer's headline gap is partly an artifact of crippling Foam-Agent to lint-only. With a tightened narrative — leading with the bottleneck analysis and the two negative findings, demoting the +7 pp / 40× framing to a precise hard-tail statement, and either fixing the comparisons (multi-seed Foam-Agent execute-mode; SE rerun matched to a hand-tuned-with-equivalent-budget control) or scoping the claims accordingly — I would lean accept. As written, the paper oversells a narrow result.

## 7. Detailed comments

- **L67 (abstract), L181, L211 (§5.1):** The "$40\times$ variance reduction" should be reported alongside the underlying counts. Concretely: "Vanilla produced 1 zero-score seed in 30 (cell × seed × task) cells; S+X produced 0." That is the operationally meaningful statement; the std ratio is a derived quantity at $n{=}3$.
- **L86–L88 (intro):** The three findings paragraph and the "two cautionary findings" paragraph are doing too much work; the per-task structure of the held-out-eval result should be in the intro since it changes how the headline numbers should be read.
- **L181–L184 (Table 1):** Consider adding a per-task win-count column ("# of held-out-eval tasks where cell beats Vanilla by ≥0.05") and a "# zero-score (cell, seed, task) cells" column. Both are computable from your existing data and would make the qualitative picture defensible without recourse to multi-seed bootstraps.
- **L207–L210 (§5.1 per-task):** Please move Table 4 (App. C per-task) to the main body, or at minimum include the seven non-rescue tasks' per-cell numbers inline. The current main-text framing buries the dependence of the headline effect on two tasks.
- **§5.4 (autonomy):** The 1/32 V0 + 1/32 V1 split is striking; please clarify whether the 2 consultations were on the same task and what was asked. A single transcript excerpt would help the reader.
- **§5.5 / Table 2 (human baseline):** The "$\ge 0.90$" cell for SIGA X+M is imprecise — replace with a single-task number from your per-task data and the corresponding seed std.
- **App. F (cross-model):** The italicised "was 0.392" pre-fix number is the right disclosure pattern. Consider adding a footnote in the abstract saying "minimax cross-model results were corrected after a system-prompt bug; see App. F" so the limitation does not surprise a reader doing a fast pass.
- **App. G (OpenFOAM, Table 7):** R+X dropping to 0.145 with 4/5 zeros on $n=1$ is too brittle to read as a property of R+X. Either run a second seed, or remove the "factor-style readout" from §5.3 and reframe as "best SIGA cell beats Vanilla on this 5-task subset."
- **App. G, "Foam-Agent baseline":** The phrase "execute-mode runs failed to yield usable benchmark outputs" needs a one-paragraph postmortem; without it a reviewer cannot tell whether Foam-Agent's native mode is *broken*, *misconfigured*, or *outperforming SIGA but in an unscorable way*.
- **App. C (Table 3):** Some cells (S+X val) have dashes for `hallucinated_extras`, `missing_block`, `structural_mismatch` — clarify whether these are zero counts or missing data; readers will assume the former.
- **App. K disclosure:** The ChatGPT-during-cluster-debugging disclosure is appropriately frank; consider adding a one-line note in §5.5 ("P1's extended-budget result includes a disclosed off-task ChatGPT visit; see App. K") so the main-text number is not read in isolation.
- **§3.1, "X conflated with S when both on":** This is mentioned in limitations; consider also reporting a clean X-without-S estimate (X+M vs Vanilla on held-out-eval). The current Table 1 contains this comparison; explicit callout would help.
- **L92, contribution (i) "held-out GEOS deck-authoring benchmark":** If you intend the 10-task held-out-eval as a community benchmark, it should be released with metric scripts and GT decks; please add a release statement and a license note.
- **Bibliography:** double-check `mcpsim2026`, `tang2026eigenagent`, `li2026agentflow`, `shi2026mdagent2`, `zhao2026polyjarvis`, `moyner2026jutulgpt` — these year-stamps suggest a heavily LLM-suggested bibtex; verify with the canonical sources before camera-ready.
- **Typography / nits:** "$8$–$36\times$" in the abstract is comparing different quantities (capped vs uncapped wall-clock); rephrase as "$\sim 7\times$ at one-hour budget; $\sim 36\times$ at uncapped extended-budget". "SIGA" in the title and "GeoAgent" in the `\sys` macro (L47, unused) — clean up before submission. App. K, "Liam / Sahchit" — looks like real names slipped past the anonymization pass; fix before the camera-ready deadline.
