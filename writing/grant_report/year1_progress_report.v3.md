# Year 1 Progress Report — Geophysics AI Agent for GEOS Multiphysics Simulation

**Project component:** UC AI team. Autonomous LLM agent for setting up coupled multiphysics subsurface simulations in GEOS (LLNL's open-source CO₂-storage and induced-seismicity simulator).

### 1. Progress toward project aims
The Year 1 aim was to deliver a working geophysics agent that lowers the expert-only barrier of authoring GEOS input decks for coupled multiphysics problems (poromechanics, thermo-poroelasticity, hydraulic fracture, wellbore flow, proppant transport). We built that agent end-to-end as a Simulator-Interface Grounding Adapter (SIGA) wrapped around a frontier coding harness, with GEOS-specific retrieval over Sphinx docs and the XSD schema, an `xmllint`-based validation hook, an agent-callable schema validator, and a compact procedural-memory primer distilled from training trajectories. We also built a held-out 46-task benchmark from GEOS advanced examples and tutorial decks (to our knowledge the first for coupled multiphysics subsurface simulation setup), scored by a tree-aware structural-similarity metric (TreeSim) decomposed across the ten canonical deck sections, and a self-evolving variant in which the harness rewrites its own primer and skill library against held-out training scores.

### 2. Key results and major accomplishments
A Resolution-IV $2^{4-1}$ factorial over the four SIGA components yields three findings against a vanilla coding-harness baseline. **Reliability:** on a hard held-out set of compound multiphysics tasks, the best SIGA configuration cuts across-seed score variance by roughly **40×**, eliminating the empty, unparseable, and missing-deck failures that the vanilla harness produces on a non-trivial tail. **Quality:** mean TreeSim on the same set rises by **~7 percentage points**, concentrated in two catastrophic-failure rescues (thermo-poroelastic wellbore: 0.355 → 0.761; proppant test: 0.541 → 0.825). **Efficiency:** the self-evolved variant matches the best hand-designed configuration with ~16% fewer tool calls. A human baseline with two graduate-level geoscientists new to GEOS finds the agent reaches the same deck-level structural similarity as a participant given an unbounded time budget (~3 hours) in ~5 minutes, an **8–36× wall-clock speedup** depending on task complexity. A cross-simulator transfer study to OpenFOAM reproduces the same pattern under a different interface and metric, and a bottleneck-failure decomposition pinpoints the remaining frontier as attribute-level semantic errors that schema validation alone cannot catch.

### 3. Obstacles, delays, or challenges
The dominant obstacle was data scarcity: the public corpus of validated coupled-multiphysics GEOS decks is small enough to rule out conventional supervised fine-tuning or RL with weight updates. We addressed this by shifting to weight-free adaptation: an offline-distilled procedural-memory primer, a self-evolving harness that improves its own skills from execution traces, and structured stop-hook feedback in lieu of gradient signal. The contribution accordingly lives at the harness rather than the model layer. No remaining blockers prevent moving forward.

### 4. Changes to aims or activities
None. The agent, benchmark, evaluation pipeline, and human baseline were all completed within scope.

### 5. Plans for next year
Two complementary tracks. **(a) Scaling data and scope with LLNL.** Deeper collaboration with the GEOS development team to gather a substantially larger corpus of validated decks, supporting both more robust evaluation and a first attempt at weight-update training once the dataset crosses threshold, and to extend the agent's responsibilities further into the geoscientist workflow with evaluation focused on autonomy and reliability in extended, multi-step tasks. **(b) Automating the recipe.** Converting the manually engineered SIGA recipe into an automated meta-procedure: given a scientific tool and its documentation, the meta-harness performs a structured pre-task study over the docs (section-level structured notes hierarchically coalesced into a persistent distilled artifact, plus a bipartite component–section grounding graph) and constructs a tool-specialized harness from that artifact, refining it through doc-anchored attribution of failures. Evaluation on GEOS plus a cross-tool transfer (FEniCS or MOOSE) to earn the meta-procedure claim, with a deliberately post-training-cutoff tool version to control for in-distribution model exposure.
